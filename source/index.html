Start with the two numbers most businesses get wrong

Most pricing mistakes are not arithmetic mistakes. They are markup-versus-margin confusion. A 40% markup sounds healthy but is really a 28.6% margin, because the profit is being measured against the larger number. That gap is where margin quietly disappears.

  • Markup divides by your cost. This is the number most people quote.
  • Margin divides by your selling price. This is the number lenders and buyers look at.
  • To hit a 30% margin you need a 42.9% markup. Most people quote 30% and end up underwater.

Common questions

What is a healthy gross margin for a small business?

It depends almost entirely on your industry. Wholesale and distribution run 10–25%. Construction trades typically run 15–30% gross but much higher net. Foodservice and retail run 25–45%. Professional services and software run 60–85%. Compare your number against your own sector, not against a general average, because a 35% margin is strong for a distributor and weak for a contractor.

How do I convert markup to margin?

Divide the markup by one plus the markup. A 40% markup becomes a 28.6% margin. A 100% markup becomes 50%. Going the other way, divide the target margin by one minus the margin: a 30% target margin requires a 42.9% markup.

Can I rely on the tax figures?

Treat them as estimates. Tax rates change and vary by city and county in ways a general formula cannot fully capture. Each tool that uses rates shows the date it was last reviewed. Verify anything that affects a filing or a contract with your state revenue department or a qualified professional.
