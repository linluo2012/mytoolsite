What makes California different

California has the highest base sales tax rate in the country. The state rate is fixed, but local add-ons vary widely by county and city, so the combined rate at checkout can be several points higher than the state rate alone.

State rate versus combined rate

The 7.25 percent figure is the statewide base rate. What a buyer actually pays is the base rate plus county, city and special district add-ons, and those vary enough within California to change the final number by several percentage points. A customer in one county may pay close to 7.25 percent while another in a different county pays more than 9.03 percent.

This matters most for businesses that quote prices without tax and add it at checkout. Using the statewide average of 1.78 percent is a reasonable planning figure, but it will be wrong at both ends of the range, and for any customer in a high-rate area the error is systematically in your favour until it is not.

Common questions

What is the sales tax rate in California?

California has a state sales tax rate of 7.25 percent. County and city add-ons average a further 1.78 percent, so the typical combined rate is about 9.03 percent. The highest combined rate in the state reaches about 12.5 percent in areas with the largest local additions.

Is California destination-based or origin-based?

California is destination-based, meaning the tax rate follows where the buyer is located rather than where the seller is based. For most goods shipped to a California customer, the destination rate applies. Exemptions exist, particularly for groceries in some states, so not every item is taxed at the standard rate.

How do I find the exact rate for my customer's address?

Statewide averages hide a wide range. A single California county can add nothing while another adds more than 5.25 percent, so two customers 30 miles apart can pay different rates. For an exact figure, look up the specific city or county at the point of sale, use the state revenue department address lookup tool, or use automated rate lookup software that resolves by customer address.

Do I need to register for sales tax in California?

Registration depends on your sales volume and whether you have physical presence in the state. A common threshold is $500,000 in annual sales. Once you exceed it, you are generally required to collect California sales tax on remote sales, register with the California revenue department, and file periodic returns even without a physical location in the state.
